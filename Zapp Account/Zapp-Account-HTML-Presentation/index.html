The bank saw an opportunity in the prepaid payments market.

PayZapp Wallet already existed within PayZapp.

But it had struggled to find a clear place in the market.

So, the bank proposed a rebrand.

To give the wallet a clearer identity and purpose.

Payment controls Tap and Pay Auto-Load

They wanted to call itZapp Account

A payment account for all your daily spends

The bank saw it as a calculated experiment with strong potential.

And the strategy was ours to define.

The problem was adoption.

Despite PayZapp's healthy user base, adoption of PayZapp Wallet was very low.

This meant looking atpeople already using PayZapp

Our Core Persona

Most PayZapp users were salaried, aged 25–40, and based in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities.

What interviews revealed

The wallet's value was unclear

Auto-Load and other capabilities went undiscovered

Wallet was a Cashpoints conversion tool

The bank was aligned.

For a strategic experiment, proving value with existing PayZapp users was the more credible first move.

Targeting a wider market

Longer discovery cycle Fierce market competition Larger delivery commitment

Adoption within PayZapp

Existing audience Faster validation Better fit for an experiment

In measurable terms, adoption meant:

Adoption ∝ Monthly spends + Monthly loads + Frequency of loads

Our findings shaped these design directions

Lead with value

Lead with the value users would feel, not the feature terminology behind it.

Center loading money

Make loading money the core action of the Zapp Account experience.

Use contextual levers

Leverage familiar PayZapp contexts to educate users even reinforce value.

Let's look at the designs

Entry points & onboarding

I knew what worked and what would stand out

  • Value-led UX copy
  • One benefit at a time
  • Recognizable 3D illustrations

The onboarding followed the same pattern

A simple carousel to introduce one value proposition at a time. It began with the benefit that brought the user in, then moved through the remaining benefits.

Most users started with limited capabilities

Limited Zapp Accounts lacked key capabilities

  • Scan and Pay for UPI transactions
  • Send money to other people
  • Transfer money to a bank account

Locked actions became upgrade opportunities

  • Scan and Pay for UPI transactions
  • Send money to other people
  • Transfer money to a bank account

Familiarity by design worked in our favor

We preserved the structure existing PayZapp users already understood.

Cashpoints reinforced an existing behavior

We kept redemption visible and tied directly to the balance because many users already treated the wallet as a Cashpoints conversion tool.

Balance became the hero

It was the first thing users came to check, so it led the page hierarchy.

Add Money became the hero CTA

Competitor patterns validated the action. Negative space and subtle motion kept it visually dominant.

Technical Challengesand Trade-offs

Loading product state

I explored a monthly budget

A self-set budget made monthly spending visible against a simple progress meter.

I explored spend insights

Monthly patterns, upcoming spends, and category cues could make the account useful beyond transactions.

I explored spending buckets

Dedicated monthly and goal-based pockets could help people reserve money before spending it.
